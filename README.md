# Seattle-Urban-Heat-Island-Project
A project that analyzes seattle's urban heat island effects, particularly in regard to HVAC and power plant contribution to heat pollusion.

This project investigates the relationship between emissions from energy generation facilities and localized urban warming, specifically focusing on the Seattle metropolitan area. Using Land Surface Temperature (LST) data from MODIS/Terra and plant operational data from the EPA's eGRID database, we hypothesize that combustion-based power plants contribute significantly to Anthropogenic Heat Release (AHR), exacerbating nighttime urban heat islands. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) confirms that nighttime LST, the more sensitive metric for AHR, is non-uniformly distributed and suggests a qualitative correlation between temperature anomalies and high-emission plant proximity. Preliminary simulated regression results validate the modeling framework. This draft outlines the project's background, methodology, initial results, and a plan for rigorous spatial regression modeling to quantify the heat island effect and its potential equity dimensions.
